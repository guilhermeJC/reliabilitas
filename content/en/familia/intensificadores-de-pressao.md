A family distinct from [[bombas|Pumps]] within the class [[adicao-de-energia|Energy Addition to the Fluid]]. While a pump **moves** liquid between process points, the intensifier **multiplies the pressure** of a fluid — its primary function is **localized pressure increase**, not volume transport.

Principle: a **differential piston** with unequal areas receives a low-pressure fluid on the large face and delivers high pressure on the small face; the intensification ratio equals the area ratio ($P_2/P_1 = A_1/A_2$).

## Usual types

*(handbooks per type in Phase 1)*

Classified by the motive fluid → intensified fluid pair:

- **Hydraulic** (oil → oil/water) — the press classic;
- **Pneumatic** (air → liquid) — test benches, air-driven;
- **Gas-gas** — pressurization of test and injection gases;
- **Water-water** — single-fluid ultra-high-pressure systems.

Typical applications: waterjet cutting, high-pressure **hydrostatic testing**, hydraulic presses and HP systems in general.

They belong to this class because they add energy (pressure) to a fluid, but they are **not** pumps in the Hydraulic Institute sense — hence they sit separately, not as a principle under Pumps.
