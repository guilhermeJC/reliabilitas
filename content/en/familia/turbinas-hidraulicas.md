Machines that **extract energy from water heads and streams** and convert it into shaft work — the functional inverse of a pump. Selection is governed by the **available head × flow** pair (specific speed).

## Usual types

*(handbooks per type in Phase 1)*

- **Pelton** — **impulse** turbine (jet): high heads, low flows;
- **Francis** — **reaction**, radial-axial flow: the world's most widespread, medium heads and flows;
- **Kaplan** — reaction, axial propeller with adjustable blades: low heads, high flows;
- **Bulb** — compact Kaplan with the generator in a submerged bulb: run-of-river plants;
- **Turgo** — impulse, inclined jet: a niche between Pelton and Francis.

In **process** plants, energy recovery from pressurized liquids usually relies on **hydraulic power recovery turbines (HPRT)** — in practice, centrifugal pumps run in reverse; ISO 14224 defines no dedicated class for hydraulic turbines (testing and acceptance follow IEC 60193, the power-generation universe).

Reliability: runner cavitation (the same physics as pump [[cavitacao|cavitation]], in the opposite direction), sediment erosion and blade fatigue dominate.
