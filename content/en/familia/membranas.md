Systems in which a **selective membrane** allows only species below a given size or molar mass to pass through. Under a pressure gradient, the permeate crosses the membrane; the concentrate is rejected. The separation scale is molecular or submicron — well beyond conventional [[filtros|Filters]].

## Usual types

Classified by separation cut-off size:

- **Microfiltration** (MF, 0.1–10 μm) — removal of bacteria, colloids.
- **Ultrafiltration** (UF, 1–100 nm) — removal of proteins, viruses.
- **Nanofiltration** (NF, 0.5–5 nm) — partial removal of monovalent salts.
- **Reverse osmosis** (RO, < 1 nm) — desalination; produces ultra-pure water.

Reliability: **fouling** (biofouling, salt scaling, colloid deposition — the dominant mode), chemical degradation by chlorine/oxidation, membrane compaction under high pressure. Central strategy: adequate pre-treatment and chemical cleaning (CIP) before fouling becomes irreversible.
